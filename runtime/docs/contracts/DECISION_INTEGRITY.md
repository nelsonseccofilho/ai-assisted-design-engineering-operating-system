# Decision identity and historical integrity

**Ownership:** SHARED_CONTRACT. **Maturity:** proposed clarification of existing persistent decision and append-only history rules.

A Decision ID identifies one subject in one declared namespace. Before assigning it, inspect the current index, standalone records, inline decision headings, aliases and concurrent changes. A title or reference alone is not an allocation mechanism.

If two subjects share an ID:
- preserve both source records and their immutable provenance;
- identify their distinct subjects and scopes before selecting canonical identities;
- allocate a unique ID through the existing owner/decision procedure;
- retain a scoped legacy alias describing which historical subject it resolves;
- append reciprocal correction notes and update the index/current references;
- do not resolve ambiguity by silently overwriting a record or assuming an alias applies to every historical occurrence.

An inline accepted decision needs an index entry or an explicit governed historical disposition. A record being present in a long handoff is not proof that the decision registry is complete. Preserve superseded decisions and distinguish historical wording from current authority.

Summaries, diagrams and generated plans must cite the applicable canonical source and preserve sequence, scope, exceptions and actual approvals. Keep canonical rule, scoped application and proposal distinct; a recommendation cannot silently become a competing contract.

A fresh-session behavioral test is separate from checking that these words exist in a document. A textual regression review proves textual coverage only.
